THE CORE CONCEPT

Operational Resilience

An organization's ability to sustain or restore critical operations when demand, supply, infrastructure, or processes are disrupted.

CAUSES

  • Dependence on a small number of suppliers or routes
  • Demand uncertainty and limited visibility
  • Capacity constraints and process bottlenecks
  • Insufficient contingency planning

CONSEQUENCES

  • Longer lead times and missed service targets
  • Higher expediting and operating costs
  • Production interruptions or stockouts
  • Reduced customer trust

INTERVENTIONS

  • Map critical suppliers, routes, and process dependencies
  • Track lead time, service level, and bottleneck utilization
  • Define contingency plans for high-impact disruptions
  • Review trade-offs using evidence and scenario planning

Three familiar moments from the world of processes, planning, and procurement.

▰
The forecast said a surge was coming.
FORECASTING

The forecast said a surge was coming. The warehouse is now fully stocked for a demand spike that exists only in the spreadsheet.

Lesson: forecasts are inputs to decisions, not facts.
●
▰
Every department hit its target.
BOTTLENECKS

Every department hit its target. The customer is still waiting because the slowest step never changed.

Lesson: optimize the whole flow, not every silo.
●
▰
We saved 5% on the unit price.
PROCUREMENT

We saved 5% on the unit price. Then paid 20% more for urgent freight.

Lesson: purchase price is not total cost.

A warehouse has excess stock of slow-moving products but keeps running out of its bestsellers. Which topic would you investigate first?

A. Inventory classification and demand forecasting   B. Office seating   C. Logo design

Answer: A. Investigate demand patterns, replenishment rules, lead times, and inventory segmentation.
