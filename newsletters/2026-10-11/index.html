Three familiar moments from the world of processes, planning, and procurement.

FORECASTING

The forecast said a surge was coming. The warehouse is now fully stocked for a demand spike that exists only in the spreadsheet.

Lesson: forecasts are inputs to decisions, not facts.
BOTTLENECKS

Every department hit its target. The customer is still waiting because the slowest step never changed.

Lesson: optimize the whole flow, not every silo.
PROCUREMENT

We saved 5% on the unit price. Then paid 20% more for urgent freight.

Lesson: purchase price is not total cost.
THE WEEKLY TAKEAWAY

Fix the system, not just the symptom.

Before asking people to work harder, examine the process, constraints, incentives, information, and feedback loops shaping their decisions.

THE 30-SECOND OPS QUIZ

A warehouse has excess stock of slow-moving products but keeps running out of its bestsellers. Which topic would you investigate first?

A. Inventory classification and demand forecasting   B. Office seating   C. Logo design

Answer: A. Investigate demand patterns, replenishment rules, lead times, and inventory segmentation.
